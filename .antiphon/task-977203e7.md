## W3 setup fragility (the 'A1 precedes B' failure)

The cause is not load. W3 drew random v4 Guids, up to 64 A notices around one B, and needed at least one below B and one above it. If B lands near either end of the uuid space, 64 draws miss that side with probability about 1/65 per side, so about 3% of runs fail. W3 now seeds fixed ids from `OrderedIds` (a random 20-hex prefix plus a 12-hex sequence, so the DB uuid order is the text order) as A1 < B < A2. It keeps both "A1 precedes B" and "A2 follows B" as real checks against the DB `OrderBy`. The overridden row id leaves the body's generated `[land <id>]` header as it was, which nothing parses back. `ContentDigest` covers the body, not the id.

## Left alone, per the brief

M12, M16 and M16b are equivalent.

M15 is an open design question. A per-pass budget, where 1 s in total is shared across all lock waits in a scan, would bound a scan with N stuck callers at about 1 s instead of N s, at the price of skipping any caller whose lock is only briefly contended late in a pass until the next scan. Is the shorter worst case worth that, given that N stuck callers is rare and each skip costs at most one 5 s scan interval?
