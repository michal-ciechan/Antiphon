## Defects

**D-1: S3 watchdog sizing ignores the planner's admission ceiling.**
- Where: plan §B S3 and the S3 budget procedure.
- Failure: `Get-NightlyProfileLaunchPlan` admits any launch estimated at up to 2400 s, or 1800 s for E2E (`nightly-tests-impl.ps1:430-446`). Its CARD-1567 comment ties that ceiling to the 60 min watchdog, and `watchdog-fit` asserts estimate < watchdog (`scripts/test-nightly-tests.ps1:1871`). In the breakdown, 80 of 386 chunks ran over 5 min and 37 over 10, so the population p95 is about 15-16 min. The proposed cap `max(2 x p95, p99 + margin)` would then be about 31-35 min. That is below the 32.3 min healthy g103 and below what the planner is still allowed to admit (40 min). The planner would launch chunks that the watchdog then kills.
- Why: a uniform population-percentile cap mixes heterogeneous chunks. "Reprice g103 first" fixes one chunk, not the rule.
- Fix: derive the cap from planned seconds per launch, with a floor, or lower the admission ceiling in the same slice. Require cap >= ceiling x measured estimate-error factor. Add a RED witness that refuses any policy where an admissible estimate times that factor reaches the cap. Cover the E2E pair (7200000 ms / 1800 s) too.

**D-2: the S6 pilot platform is unpinned.**
- Where: plan §C and the lane roster line.
- Failure: only CP-2 and CP-4 are pinned to Windows. The 657.5 min opportunity is Windows-measured: Job Object, ConPTY and process-spawn costs. The named roster cases `NightlyNativeOwnershipTests.C1039_DescendantExit` and `C1039_DrainBeforeReturn` throw `SkipTestException` off Windows (`NightlyNativeOwnershipTests.cs:36-38`). Under default routing, which prefers server2, the pilot either breaks its own "zero required skips" rule or quietly drops the native cases. Either way it measures the wrong OS.
- Fix: pin S6 to `-Platform Windows`, record the platform in the trial receipt, and give Linux pilot evidence no credit.

**D-3: the S6 census misses tests that assert elapsed time.**
- Where: §C trial protocol, steps 1-2.
- Failure: the census lists fixed real-time waits, but not tests that assert an upper bound on elapsed time or overhead.
  - `TestTimingChokePointTests.C1514T_Overhead` is `[ParallelLimiter<ProcessSpawnLimit>]`. Its only other constraint is `[NotInParallel("C1514Choke")]` (`TestTimingChokePointTests.cs:10-11`), so at width 2 it can share the host with any other strict test.
  - It asserts wall and CPU medians (`:232-233`) and is known to go red when co-run under load.
  - A heuristic grep finds about 18 strict-limiter files with similar elapsed bounds.
- Fix: add elapsed/ratio bounds to the census; such classes stay width 1 or need a reason. Put `C1514T_Overhead` in the roster, or name it as a promotion obligation.

**D-4: the S6 acceptance rule does not hold up statistically at N=10.**
- Where: §C trial protocol, step 4.
- Failure:
  - The nearest-rank p95 of 10 samples is simply the maximum. With an uncontrolled 5 quiet / 5 loaded split, each load level has n=5.
  - "No individual test p95 regression over 10%" measures the expected effect of contention: per-test latency rises at width 2 while total wall falls. The rule would either reject every useful candidate or get waived ad hoc.
  - Zero failures in 10 runs only bounds the per-run new-flake rate below about 26% at 95% confidence (rule of three).
- Fix:
  - Judge safety by each test's duration against its own deadline (deadline margin), plus a per-load-level wall comparison.
  - State the detectable flake rate explicitly.
  - Report per-test p95 descriptively only, unless n is raised.
