## Ordering answer: unrun-first needs no code change and no second seed

`New-NightlyDispatchSchedule` (scripts/lib/nightly-dispatch.ps1) receives the seed as
`PreviousRun`. `Get-NightlyPreviousRunKind` returns `operator-seed`, and
`Resolve-NightlyStoppedSeedOrder` (scripts/lib/nightly-stopped-run-seed.ps1) orders the new
cut's chunks by class, not by chunk name. It uses five bands:

0. **Failed.** A chunk holding a seed `failedTests` method, or with no node list and a class
   that failed. Within the band, oldest `lastRunAtUtc` comes first.
1. **Not completed in the seed.** Each lane is rotated to start at its first interrupted chunk,
   or after its last passed chunk, and wraps around. These are taken in two passes:
   - **interrupted**: started but not completed. Every lane's interrupted chunks come first.
   - **untouched**: `started=false`, plus chunks whose classes the seed never saw, such as new
     classes, or `client` and `scripts`.
2. **Siblings.** Other chunks of a class whose failed method is placed elsewhere.
3. **Passed.** Every class completed with no failure, oldest `lastRunAtUtc` first.

What the seed format carries:

- `chunks[]`: suite, chunk, classes, `completed`, `started`, `lastRunAtUtc`.
- `failedTests[]`: suite, chunk, className, method and outcome. The outcome must be
  Failed, Error, Timeout or Aborted.

The loader does not accept a chunk-level "prior failure" entry. It does not need one: the
stopped-run export already writes this run's 289 unrun chunks as `started=false,
completed=false`. Merge copies the newest seed's chunk rows unchanged, so the seed above
already makes them band 1. Marking unrun chunks as failed would mean writing fake
`failedTests` rows. That would be hacking the seed, and it would gain nothing.
