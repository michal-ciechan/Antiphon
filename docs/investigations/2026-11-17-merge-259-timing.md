The C1349 ×20 requirement alone cost 54 minutes (18.1 running plus 36.0 waiting across its 20

minutes (not 09:3x). Its model time was 20.9 minutes and its slot holds totaled 3,508 s, so it had

### Three cheapest changes that keep every gate

The candidates are ranked by minutes saved per unit of effort.

1. **Gate the C1349 repeat block once, or run it in one process.**
   - The 20 lost-wakeup invocations each took their own load gate, which cost 36 minutes of waiting and about 8 minutes of process start-up (20 × about 25 s).
   - Gating once and running the 20 back to back would recover up to 36 minutes, at the same 20/20 evidence and the same threshold.
   - A single-process repeat (the compile-time `AntiphonCheckpointRepeat` path) would also recover the start-up minutes. It changes the evidence shape (one process instead of 20), and that is the operator's call.
2. **Poll the load gate every 15 to 30 s instead of 180 s, and do not take the first sample right after the merge's own heavy row.**
   - Each of the 17 waits was quantized to 3 minutes, and 12 of the 13 row waits ended on a reading of 9.85 or lower (the other on 11.22).
   - The saving is up to about 2.5 minutes per episode, at most about 40 minutes. A realistic estimate is half the poll on average, about 25 minutes. The true crossing times are **UNOBSERVED**.
   - Waiting 30 to 60 s after its own 3 to 5 minute rows would avoid self-induced readings like 29.59 and 29.16. The threshold of 16 stays.
3. **Count CP-6 and `DerivedImpactIndexTests` from the guard-union TRX instead of re-running them.**
   - CP-6's nine classes and `DerivedImpactIndexTests` are both inside the 11-class guard union, which already ran with `--report-trx`.
   - Re-running them cost 4.6 minutes of running and 3.0 minutes of waiting, with every test still executed once. The brief's "DerivedImpactIndexTests alone" wording would need to change.

### Candidates evaluated and not ranked

- **Warm worktree slots (CARD-0824) or a pre-built shared test binary.** The merge's truly cold build was 3.6 minutes. That is faster than the 242 to 366 s warm-ish checkpoint builds, and the brief had estimated 15 to 30 minutes. The most a warm build could save is about 3.5 minutes, roughly 2 %. Peers are where this matters: fc32e512 alone did 14 cold builds totaling 4,899 s, and those builds produced the load the merge waited on.
- **A smaller batch.** Batch 258 had 56 picks and took 112 minutes. Batch 259 had 14 picks and took 159. Pick count did not drive the time; the row count and the load did.
- **Running independent rows concurrently.** No row ever waited for a slot, and the limit was CPU load. Running rows concurrently raises the load that the gate measures, so it would not help while the gate stays at 16. Rows with no textual DB, fixture or WebApplicationFactory reference (a grep of each class file, not a dependency proof) are `TestTimingOperationLabelTests`, `ImpactedClassesSelectorTests`, `MergeHighRadiusSubsetTests`, `DerivedImpactIndexTests`, `DockerStackDocumentationTests` and most of the guard classes. Every row still takes a build slot under the CARD-0589 rule.
- **Host scheduling.** Holding new peer cold builds while a Merge is verifying is the change with the largest effect: about 85 minutes of waiting traced to peer builds. It is a dispatch-policy decision rather than a cheap change.
- **The land-graph 5 s copy budget.** It refused at loads 8 to 10 and cost about 11 minutes, including 6.2 minutes of waiting and 1.7 minutes of compaction. Recalibrating it for 10,884 tracked files belongs to its owner card.
