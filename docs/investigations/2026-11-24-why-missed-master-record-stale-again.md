## Why publication let the records drift

- CARD-1753 provides `land-graph-land-refresh --base <batch-base>` as a caller-run **pre-land** step. It refreshes changes owned by the batch and warns on an inherited mismatch when the batch leaves selected records alone. The server's verification policy uses the graph to decide whether to build. It has no corresponding write step. A direct push skips the caller-run refresh, and a later unrelated land can carry the inherited digest forward. [Standing rule](../orchestration-loop.md#land-graph-refresh) · [Verification policy](../../server/Application/Services/LandVerificationPolicy.cs)
- FT-5 implements index regeneration in the server's detached land worktree **only after an index-only rebase conflict**. A clean rebase takes the normal `Prepared` path. A direct push never reaches the conflict handler. The documented wave refresh covers the other flows when a caller runs it. The post-land early-warning lane reads the selector and runs selected tests. It has no artifact publication step. [Land protocol](../../server/Application/Services/AgentTaskLandingProtocol.cs#L293) · [Landing wave procedure](../orchestration-loop.md#fast-track-lane-operator-2026-11-20) · [Early-warning planner](../../scripts/lib/Antiphon.Nightly/PostLandEarlyWarning.cs)

## Smallest durable change

Add a master-only step after rebase and before the land operation pins `Prepared`. Compare the prepared tree with the fetched target. For a selected definition or pins change, reuse the graph refresh and approval machinery, preserving its refusals for unknown constructs and changed pins. For changes to index inputs, including tracked-path moves and deletions, call the existing `RegenerateIndexAsync` from the successful preparation path as well as the index-conflict path. Publish the source and any generated commits together through the existing target-race retry. A moved target restarts preparation and recomputes both outputs against the new parent. This closes the clean-rebase gap and avoids a window with stale master bytes. Route routine direct-push batches through the same preparation step before their fast-forward, or require its result at the protected ref. A direct push that bypasses preparation otherwise retains the same failure mode.
