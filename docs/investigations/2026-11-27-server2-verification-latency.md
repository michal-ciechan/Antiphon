# Server2 verification latency on 2026-11-27

Builds consumed **74% of sampled build-slot time**. The longest completed build lease in the sample was **21m05s**. One nine-second test row waited nine minutes. Disk exhaustion caused real build, test and receipt-write failures in the morning. The broker backlog and confinement refusals continued after disk recovery. The four-slot limit and six-worker grant are live settings. There is enough evidence to avoid raising the slot count, but no controlled comparison to choose a new limit.

## What held the slots

The shared broker's `GET /build-slots` returned four enabled slots, `maxCpuCount=6`, and a 16,384 MiB memory floor. At 13:58 UTC the broker had four leases and four waiters with 94,076 MiB available. At 14:01 it had four leases and six waiters with at least 85,554 MiB available throughout a six-sample capture. One snapshot showed two separate builds from the same Review task owning two of the four slots. This is a per-invocation budget across worktrees and runners using the shared authority. The standing runner's local count reported zero while the shared broker was full. [Live snapshots](/work/evidence/task-a6eed88b/broker-samples.jsonl) · [Host stats capture](/work/evidence/task-a6eed88b/api-1.json) · [Shared broker settings](../../docker-compose.server2-runner.yml)

The broker queues by arrival and admits a polling waiter whose position fits the number of free slots. Waiters poll at a 15-second default, and the broker drops a waiter after 60 seconds of silence. With multiple free slots, an eligible poll can arrive ahead of another eligible poll. There is no build duration, test duration or per-task weighting. The 14:01:54 and 14:02:00 snapshots showed one vacancy with six waiters still listed. The head build took the vacancy by 14:02:06, and the `CP-fix-green` waiter advanced from second to first. A vacancy can sit between polls without a live holder. ## Host and disk attribution

Server2 has 24 logical CPUs (12 physical cores with SMT). Four six-node MSBuild grants can use all 24 nominal worker positions by themselves. A node count caps MSBuild scheduling, and the compiler, child builds, tests, server, DIND and agent CLI work also take CPU and I/O. The 13:28–13:58 live series has 360 five-second samples. One-minute load median was 35.67, p95 61.64 and peak 70.84. Over the same interval the runner reports about 62% mean CPU and 95% peak. A 25-second `vmstat` sample at 14:01 found 0–4% I/O wait, 15–44% CPU idle and up to 19 blocked processes. It shows bursts and contention, with insufficient coverage to diagnose any individual read. ## Why the zero-test rows refused

`BuildConfinement.ListUntracked` explicitly includes Git-ignored files outside declared write roots. It also stamps tracked files. The independent Git source observation can report a clean checkout while this ledger has unreadable ignored inputs. The scheduled build captures the ledger before requesting a slot, validates changes and known unreadables at admission, and rechecks content after the lease. The guarded reader inspects the actual open handle, requires a single link and fails closed after three seconds without progress. Linux already runs the regular-file syscall on the reader's dedicated thread. The retained messages establish the progress deadline, but do not distinguish scheduling delays from time in a filesystem call. ## Recommendations

1. **Restore broker and host history telemetry first.** During a drained window, verify the deployed pin and qualify the broker with `/metrics` and `/host-load` against the shared authority. Repair or activate the server2 history binding and confirm stored samples. Retain build and row grant/release/reap events by task and lease. Add separate preflight, actual compiler, verification, test and cleanup phases to the incident sample. This gives a fleet p95 and utilization denominator before tuning. Replacing a live broker can lose lease state, so require a verified empty transition. Capture CPU, I/O wait and disk free space at the same timestamps as the lease phases.
2. **Keep four slots for the baseline, then trial fewer workers per heavy build.** Ask the operator to compare the current `4 × 6` to `4 × 4` at comparable task/rc mix. This removes eight nominal MSBuild worker positions while preserving four admission seats. Compare p95 wait and wall, completed leases/hour, CPU/load, per-build peak memory and zero-test refusals. A four-worker build may run longer under light load. Roll back a net throughput or latency regression. Test three slots separately only if contention remains high. Both the queue and contention are real, so increasing slots without lowering per-build demand risks longer service times and a longer queue.
3. **Avoid duplicate builds first.** Correlate each run's actual project, properties, source fingerprint and cache disposition. Within a task, group exact same-input rows around a single source-bound producer and use verified warm replay when the policy permits. Removing one upper-tail build shelters roughly 12–21 slot-minutes and its disk writes. Distinct SHAs or differing properties may still require a build. For cross-worktree reuse, prototype immutable, independently verified artifacts published by one producer and materialized into isolated consumers. Key on source SHA and fingerprint, full build inputs and toolchain, runtime/SDK, OS and architecture. Measure absolute-path and generated-input sensitivity, identity checks and cache lifetime before treating a hit as a valid receipt. Sharing mutable `bin`/`obj` or hard-linking output into a worktree would introduce source and custody risks.
4. **Experiment with bounded short-row admission after telemetry is available.** Consider an explicit class for verified no-build rows, with aging for other work and a shared cap. If a slot is reserved, allow long work to borrow it while the short class is empty. Also measure a per-task cap when different tasks are queued. A short row can currently wait 9 minutes for under 10 seconds of work. A protected lane would cut that tail when its short demand fits, at a cost to build throughput when busy and with starvation risk if duration claims are wrong. A head-of-queue push notification could also reclaim some of each observed 15-second polling gap. Qualify scheduling and renewal before changing fairness.
5. **Reduce avoidable confinement work and report permanent preflight refusals earlier.** Prefer clean producer checkouts and the existing owned-output cleanup paths. Commission a narrow pre-admission check for *already observed*, terminal unreadables, with the same path evidence and no compiler launch or reusable-cache invalidation. In `d74f1eb4` this could remove 7m15s of futile admission delay. Avoid an unconditional `node_modules` exclusion or a longer read deadline. Any future generated-root exclusion needs explicit ownership, proof that the checkpoint cannot consume or mutate that root, and separate tracked, ignored, link and concurrent-writer witnesses. Early refusal would still leave the initial scan cost. For stalled reads, collect per-phase timestamps and host scheduler/storage evidence before altering the failure budget.
6. **Keep disk headroom and measure cleanup as its own capacity cost.** Correlate DIND image and volume growth, worktree output and session/temp retention with disk alerts. Reuse the owner-checked retention preview described by the disk follow-up. Escalate sustained growth before fixture or receipt writes fail. Look separately at cleanup enumeration/custody and deletion time in `13129357`. Its 9m31s is a large off-slot saving opportunity if repeatable. Concurrency in cleanup could increase filesystem contention, so profile before changing it.
